What is the Gospel

The good news, in the words the apostles used to pass it on.

The Apostolic Formula

The earliest summary of the Christian message comes from Paul, writing to the church in Corinth around AD 55, quoting a creed scholars date to within a few years of the crucifixion itself:

“For I delivered to you as of first importance what I also received, that Christ died for our sins according to the Scriptures, and that He was buried, and that He was raised on the third day according to the Scriptures.”

1 Corinthians 15:3–4

Four historical claims sit at the center of the gospel: Christ died, He was buried, He was raised, and He appeared. Everything else in Christian teaching builds on this foundation.

The Problem: Sin and Separation

The gospel begins with a diagnosis. Scripture teaches that every person has fallen short of God's standard:

“for all have sinned and fall short of the glory of God,”

Romans 3:23

Sin is not merely a moral failing but a relational rupture — it separates people from a holy God, and its wages are death:

“For the wages of sin is death, but the free gift of God is eternal life in Christ Jesus our Lord.”

Romans 6:23

The Solution: Christ's Death and Resurrection

Where humanity could not meet God's standard, Christian teaching holds that God met it on humanity's behalf. Jesus, said to be fully God and fully man, lived a sinless life and died a substitutionary death in the place of sinners:

“But God demonstrates His own love toward us, in that while we were yet sinners, Christ died for us.”

Romans 5:8

His resurrection three days later is presented not as a metaphor but as a historical event that vindicated His claims and defeated death itself — the subject explored further on the Resurrection page.

The Response: Faith, Not Works

The gospel's claim is that reconciliation with God comes by grace through faith, not by earning it:

“For by grace you have been saved through faith; and that not of yourselves, it is the gift of God; not as a result of works, so that no one may boast.”

Ephesians 2:8–9

“that if you confess with your mouth Jesus as Lord, and believe in your heart that God raised Him from the dead, you will be saved;”

Romans 10:9
