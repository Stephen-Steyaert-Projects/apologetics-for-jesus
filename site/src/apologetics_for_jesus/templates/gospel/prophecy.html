Fulfilled Prophecy

Centuries before His birth, the Old Testament described a coming Messiah in striking detail.

What Is Messianic Prophecy?

Scattered across the Hebrew Scriptures — written across many centuries by many different authors — is a recurring portrait of a coming figure: a king, a servant, and a redeemer. Christians hold that Jesus of Nazareth fulfilled this portrait in specific, historically verifiable ways, and that the sheer number and specificity of these predictions is itself evidence that His life was not coincidental.

Born in Bethlehem

“But you, Bethlehem Ephrathah, too little to be among the clans of Judah, from you One will go forth for Me to be ruler in Israel.”

Micah 5:2

Written roughly 700 years before Jesus' birth, this prophecy names the specific town where the Messiah would be born — not the more prominent Jerusalem, but the small village of Bethlehem. The Gospel writers record Jesus' birth there, noting that it occurred during a Roman census (Luke 2:1–7; Matthew 2:1).

The Suffering Servant

“But He was pierced through for our transgressions, He was crushed for our iniquities; the chastening for our well-being fell upon Him, and by His scourging we are healed.”

Isaiah 53:5

Isaiah 53 describes a righteous servant who suffers and dies on behalf of others — despised, rejected, silent before His accusers, and ultimately numbered among criminals in death. Christians see this chapter as a direct portrait of the crucifixion, written some seven centuries before crucifixion existed as a Roman practice.

Betrayed for Thirty Pieces of Silver

“And I said to them, ‘If it is good in your sight, give me my wages; but if not, never mind!’ So they weighed out thirty shekels of silver as my wages.”

Zechariah 11:12

Zechariah names the exact price at which the shepherd-figure is valued and betrayed. Matthew's Gospel records that Judas Iscariot was paid this same amount — thirty pieces of silver — to betray Jesus (Matthew 26:15), and that the money was later used to purchase a potter's field, another detail Zechariah and Jeremiah anticipate (Matthew 27:3–10).

The Statistical Argument

Apologists have long pointed to the improbability of one person fulfilling so many independent predictions by chance. In Science Speaks, mathematician Peter Stoner calculated the odds of a single individual randomly fulfilling just eight of these Messianic prophecies at roughly 1 in 1017 — a number Stoner illustrated by imagining the state of Texas covered two feet deep in silver dollars, one of them marked, with a blindfolded man reaching down to pick the marked coin on his first try (Stoner). Skeptics respond that the calculation assumes the prophecies are independent of one another and that the Gospel writers could not have shaped their accounts to match known prophetic expectations — an objection worth weighing seriously, and one addressed further in the historical case made on the Resurrection page.
